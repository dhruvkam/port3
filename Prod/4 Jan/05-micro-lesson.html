Lesson #1

Why most dashboards fail

And a simple framework to fix them

📖 90 seconds to read • 💡 1 concept to remember

Most dashboards are built to show data. They should be built to enable decisions.

The difference? A data-display dashboard asks "what metrics exist?" A decision-enabling dashboard asks "what question is someone trying to answer?"

12 metrics
→
Cognitive overload
→
No decision
The typical dashboard spiral

When I rebuilt Walmart's finance dashboards, we cut metrics from 12 to 3. Result: 3x faster decisions.

The instinct is to add more. More filters. More views. More data. But every addition is a decision the user has to make before they can make the decision they came for.

The test

Can someone use your dashboard without training? If not, the dashboard is the problem—not the user.

The best dashboards disappear. They deliver an answer and get out of the way. The user doesn't admire the interface—they make a decision and move on.
